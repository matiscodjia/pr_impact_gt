\title{Reference Imperfection Can Reverse Model Rankings\\
in Segmentation Evaluation}
\author{Matis Codjia}
\supervisor{Élodie Puybareau (team TIRF), Marie Puren (team MNSHS)}
\date{July 2026}
\revision{v1.0}
\email{matis.codjia@epita.fr}

\summary{
Deep-learning segmentation models are trained \emph{and evaluated} against manual
annotations that are themselves imperfect: inter-observer disagreement on tubular
structures such as pulmonary vessels is well documented. We ask a question that is
usually left implicit: \emph{if the reference is noisy, does our model ranking survive
it?} We introduce a formal, calibrated annotation-noise operator
$\gtminus = \Nop(\gtstar;\theta)$ parametrised by a family, a scale $r$, a severity
$p$, a directional bias $\mu$, and a seed, calibrate it to an inter-observer agreement
window taken from the literature -- no per-case multi-annotator data exists for PARSE, a
limitation we state plainly and design around -- and study, on $85$ out-of-fold cases of
PARSE pulmonary arteries with nnU-Net, how the resulting reference imperfection moves
evaluation scores \emph{and rankings}. Our central, demonstrated result is not a
magnitude comparison but a reversal: changing only the reference, from the clean
$\gtstar$ to a distal-omission degraded $\gtminus$, \emph{flips which of two
independently trained networks scores best}, on two leaderboard-standard metrics (HD95,
NSD@2mm), for both competitors tested against the clean-trained model. This is not a
fragile point estimate: a per-case interaction test (paired Wilcoxon on the
difference-of-differences, Benjamini--Hochberg FDR) reaches $p_{\text{FDR}}$ as low as
$2\times10^{-10}$, and the reversed order is recovered in $100\%$ of $10\,000$
case-resamplings. We give the reversal a mechanism, not just a measurement: the
clean-trained network still finds the distal branches that omission deletes from the
reference; once the reference no longer contains them, those branches score as distant
false positives and inflate HD95, while the noise-matched network, which never predicts
them either, is rewarded for reproducing the reference's own error -- and clDice,
dominated by the intact proximal trunk, does not move. A magnitude comparison between the
reference effect and the model effect remains useful context (median $2.3\times$ across
$54$ metric/scenario/model-pair triples, several combinations even below $1\times$), but
we no longer treat it as the paper's argument: a reversal need not be large to invalidate
a ranking. The magnitudes involved are sub-voxel to half-voxel ($0.12$--$0.37$mm HD95),
which could read as academic until anchored: on the PARSE 2022 pulmonary-artery
leaderboard, the same anatomy at comparable resolution, the HD95 margin separating the
top two real submissions is $0.16$--$0.61$mm -- the same order of magnitude as our
reversal, not a metaphor but a magnitude comparison against real competing entries. None of
our predictions were post-processed, and HD95 is the statistic most exposed to a single
isolated fragment ($N(P)\in[44,81]$ components) -- so we tested the obvious confound
directly: the reversal survives largest-connected-component post-processing intact on both
metrics, at a real but asymmetric clDice cost (negligible for the clean-trained model, real
for the noise-trained ones), ruling out unprocessed fragmentation as the explanation. We
report, alongside, a second and independent pathology: the Betti-$0$ error $|\Delta N|$ is
blind to component correspondence and moves incoherently under the same reference change --
visible in the same data but, at this sample size, not statistically established, which we
report as such rather than overstate. We close with measurement-practice recommendations
and a direction for what we did not measure: correspondence-aware topological metrics.}

\frenchsummary{
Les modèles de segmentation par apprentissage profond sont entraînés \emph{et
évalués} contre des annotations manuelles elles-mêmes imparfaites~; le désaccord
inter-observateur sur les structures tubulaires comme les vaisseaux pulmonaires est bien
documenté. Nous posons une question souvent laissée implicite~: \emph{si la référence est
bruitée, notre classement de modèles y survit-il~?} Nous introduisons un opérateur de
bruit d'annotation formel et calibré, $\gtminus=\Nop(\gtstar;\theta)$, doté d'un unique
axe de biais~$\mu$, le calibrons sur une fenêtre d'accord inter-observateur issue de la
littérature~-- faute de données multi-annotateurs sur PARSE, une limitation que nous
assumons et contournons explicitement~-- puis étudions, sur $85$ cas \emph{out-of-fold}
d'artères pulmonaires PARSE avec nnU-Net, comment cette imperfection de la référence
déplace les scores \emph{et les classements}. Notre résultat central et démontré n'est
pas une comparaison de magnitude mais un retournement~: changer la seule référence, de
la référence propre $\gtstar$ vers une référence dégradée par omission $\gtminus$,
\emph{inverse lequel de deux réseaux entraînés indépendamment obtient le meilleur
score}, sur deux métriques de classement standard (HD95, NSD@2mm), pour les deux
concurrents testés contre le modèle entraîné propre. Ce n'est pas un point estimé
fragile~: un test d'interaction par cas (Wilcoxon apparié sur la différence des
différences, FDR de Benjamini--Hochberg) atteint un $p_{\text{FDR}}$ aussi bas que
$2\times10^{-10}$, et l'ordre inversé est retrouvé dans $100\%$ des $10\,000$
rééchantillonnages de cas. Nous donnons un mécanisme au retournement, pas seulement une
mesure~: le réseau entraîné propre détecte toujours les branches distales que
l'omission retire de la référence~; une fois la référence privée de ces branches,
celles-ci comptent comme de lointains faux positifs et font exploser le HD95, tandis que
le réseau entraîné sur le même bruit, qui ne les prédit pas non plus, est récompensé
pour avoir reproduit l'erreur de la référence~-- et le clDice, dominé par le tronc
proximal intact, ne bouge pas. Une comparaison de magnitude entre effet référence et
effet modèle reste un contexte utile (ratio médian $2{,}3\times$ sur $54$ triplets
métrique/scénario/paire de modèles, plusieurs combinaisons même sous $1\times$), mais
nous ne la traitons plus comme l'argument du papier~: un retournement n'a pas besoin
d'être grand pour invalider un classement. Les magnitudes en jeu sont sous-voxel à
demi-voxel ($0{,}12$--$0{,}37$mm de HD95), ce qui pourrait sembler académique sans
ancrage~: sur le classement public PARSE 2022, même anatomie, résolution comparable, la
marge de HD95 séparant les deux meilleures soumissions réelles est de $0{,}16$--$0{,}61$mm
-- le même ordre de grandeur que notre retournement, pas une métaphore mais une
comparaison de magnitude contre de vraies entrées concurrentes. Aucune de nos
prédictions n'a été post-traitée, et HD95 est la statistique la plus exposée à un
fragment isolé ($N(P)\in[44,81]$ composantes)~-- nous avons donc testé directement ce
biais évident~: le retournement survit intact au post-traitement par plus grande
composante connexe sur les deux métriques, à un coût en clDice réel mais asymétrique
(négligeable pour le modèle propre, réel pour les modèles bruités), ce qui écarte la
fragmentation non traitée comme explication. Nous rapportons, en parallèle, une seconde
pathologie indépendante~: l'erreur de Betti-$0$ ($|\Delta N|$), aveugle à la
correspondance des composantes, se déplace de façon incohérente sous le même changement
de référence~-- visible sur les mêmes données mais, à cette taille d'échantillon, non
établie statistiquement, ce que nous rapportons tel quel plutôt que de le
surinterpréter. Nous concluons par des recommandations de pratique de mesure et une
direction pour ce que nous n'avons pas mesuré~: des métriques topologiques sensibles à
la correspondance.}

\chapter{Introduction}
% =====================================================================
Supervised image segmentation rests on a silent assumption: that the manual
annotation used as ground truth (\gtstar) is correct. In practice annotations are
produced by humans under time pressure, vary between observers, and are especially
unreliable on thin, branching structures where a one-voxel disagreement on the vessel
wall is a large fraction of the lumen. The same imperfect annotations are used to
\emph{train} models and, more consequentially, to \emph{evaluate} and \emph{rank}
them. When a benchmark declares model~$A$ better than model~$B$ by a small margin on
a metric computed against a noisy reference, we rarely ask whether that ranking
survives the noise.

The stakes are asymmetric. In training, imperfect labels are one nuisance among many,
partly absorbed by regularisation and augmentation. In evaluation, the reference is the
\emph{definition} of correctness: there is no appeal beyond it. A metric does not measure
how good a segmentation is; it measures how close it is to a particular human's tracing.
If that tracing is systematically off -- because a protocol, a tool default, or an
individual habit pushes every contour the same way -- then the metric faithfully rewards
agreement with the error. The danger is not random disagreement, which averages out, but
the \emph{consistent} kind, which does not. This distinction, long noted qualitatively
\cite{heller2018imperfect}, is the axis along which we organise everything that follows.

This report makes that question explicit and tractable. We do not study a new
architecture; we study the \emph{measurement apparatus} of segmentation research --
the annotation and the metric -- and the conditions under which it produces
trustworthy verdicts. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item A \textbf{formal annotation-noise operator} $\Nop(\gtstar;\theta)$ with a small,
  interpretable parameter vector and a single continuous \textbf{bias axis $\mu$} that
  interpolates between near-unbiased jitter and systematic, learnable bias. We calibrate it to
  an inter-observer window \emph{taken from the literature} -- we have no multi-annotator PARSE
  data, and we design around this rather than hide it (Sec.~\ref{sec:formalism}).
  \item A \textbf{magnitude result}: on $85$ OOF cases, changing only the reference moves a
  fixed network's score more than changing the model does ($0.10$ vs.\ $\le0.02$ in clDice), so
  the reference is a larger source of variation in a ranking than the algorithm
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:refvsmodel}).
  \item A \textbf{negative methodological finding}: the Betti-$0$ error $|\Delta N|$ is
  \emph{pathological} under an imperfect reference -- correspondence-blind, it can report
  near-perfect topology for two mutually wrong objects -- which disqualifies it as the
  connectivity instrument in exactly this setting and motivates correspondence-aware
  alternatives (Sec.~\ref{sec:betti}).
  \item An explicit \textbf{instrument-matching methodology} and its measured (largely
  by-construction) \textbf{metric dissociation}, together with an honest account of scope: what
  we measured, what is tautological (the clean/clean baseline), and what remains open (learned
  bias, Q3) (Secs.~\ref{sec:methodology},~\ref{sec:results},~\ref{sec:future}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Roadmap.} Sec.~\ref{sec:related} situates the work. Sec.~\ref{sec:formalism} defines
the operator, its families, and the bias axis $\mu$. Sec.~\ref{sec:methodology} states the
instrument-matching methodology, the calibration, and the design. Sec.~\ref{sec:results} reports
the calibration/dissociation, the Betti-$0$ pathology, the reference-vs-model magnitude result,
and the sanity bound. Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} and~\ref{sec:ethics} draw the (measured) lessons,
Sec.~\ref{sec:future} the open directions, and Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations} the boundaries.

\chapter{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================
\section{Imperfect and noisy labels}
Heller et al.~\cite{heller2018imperfect} systematically perturbed segmentation labels and
reported the counter-intuitive result that networks are remarkably robust to
\emph{unbiased}, zero-mean boundary noise -- displacements cancel across slices and the
model recovers the mean boundary -- whereas \emph{systematic} biases (consistent
under-segmentation, omissions) genuinely degrade performance. This biased/unbiased
dichotomy is the conceptual seed of our study; our contribution is to make it a
\emph{continuous, calibrated} knob ($\mu$) rather than a qualitative contrast. A broader
literature on learning with noisy labels~\cite{karimi2020noisy} develops loss functions
and training schemes robust to label corruption, but treats noise mostly as a training-time
nuisance to be overcome. We are concerned with the complementary and less-studied
\emph{evaluation-time} role of the same imperfection: what it does to rankings, not only to
training.

\section{Boundary perturbation and label-noise models}
Simple morphological erosion/dilation is spatially uniform and structurally unrealistic: it
moves every boundary voxel by the same amount regardless of local geometry. Markov-process
boundary perturbations~\cite{yao2023markov} and the GSD-Net line~\cite{gsdnet2025} distort
contours irregularly and, crucially, allow a foreground-reducing ($S_R$) or
foreground-expanding ($S_E$) bias -- the direction our $\mu$ formalises. Our
\texttt{boundary\_drift} family follows this idea with a smoothed, spatially correlated
displacement field superimposed on a constant drift, so that the systematic and the
stochastic parts of a boundary error are separable by construction
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift}). Distinct from these, our \texttt{distal\_omission} family models a
\emph{topological} error -- the deletion of whole thin branches -- which no boundary-only
model reproduces.

\section{Topology-aware and surface metrics}
clDice~\cite{shit2021cldice} measures the overlap of morphological \emph{skeletons} and is
the standard for tubular connectivity; by construction it is insensitive to a boundary
displacement that leaves the centreline intact -- a fact that becomes central to our
instrument-matching argument, and a trap when it is used to certify a boundary perturbation
as harmless. The Betti-number error complements it by counting connected-component
mismatches. On the surface side, the Normalised Surface Dice (NSD)~\cite{nikolov2018nsd}
measures agreement within a tolerance and the 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95)
bounds the worst geometric deviation; both are \emph{unsigned}, and both lose resolution
below the voxel -- properties we quantify directly in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. Recent
community efforts on metric selection and its pitfalls~\cite{maierhein2024metrics} argue
precisely that metric choice must follow the claim; our instrument--noise pairing is a
concrete, testable instance of that principle for the imperfect-reference setting.

\section{Reference construction and inter-observer variability}
Where multiple annotations exist, label-fusion methods such as
STAPLE~\cite{warfield2004staple} estimate a consensus reference and per-rater performance,
implicitly acknowledging that no single tracing is ground truth. Probabilistic segmentation
models~\cite{kohl2018probunet} go further and predict a \emph{distribution} of plausible
segmentations, reflecting annotator ambiguity rather than collapsing it. These lines treat
variability as something to model or average; we treat its \emph{biased} component as
something that can be measured, can penalise unfairly, and can be learned.

\section{Framework and data}
We use nnU-Net~\cite{isensee2021nnunet} -- a strong, self-configuring baseline that removes
architecture choice as a confound -- on the PARSE pulmonary-artery
dataset~\cite{parse2022}, a CT benchmark whose sub-millimetre distal vessels make it an
extreme and informative stress test for the surface/topology coupling.

\section{The annotation-noise operator}
Let $\gtstar\subseteq\Omega$ be a clean binary annotation on a voxel grid $\Omega$
with anisotropic spacing $s=(s_1,s_2,s_3)$ mm. A degraded annotation is the image of a
stochastic operator
\begin{equation}
  \gtminus \;=\; \Nop(\gtstar;\theta),
  \qquad
  \theta=(\varphi,\,r,\,p,\,\mu,\,\text{seed}),
\end{equation}
where $\varphi$ selects a \emph{family} (mechanism), $r$ is a \emph{scale}
(radius/amplitude/length), $p$ a \emph{severity} (fraction or per-unit probability),
$\mu$ a \emph{directional bias} (boundary family only), and the seed fixes the random
realisation, making $\Nop$ reproducible to the voxel. \textbf{Application policy:}
$\Nop$ is applied to \emph{every} image (and every training batch); $p$ is never a
probability of applying the operator, only a severity. Code-level guarantees ensure at
least one structural unit or surface voxel is affected whenever $p>0$.

\section{Families}
We use two complementary mechanisms (a third, \texttt{distal\_truncation}, is
discarded as redundant with omission; see Sec.~\ref{sec:calib}).

\paragraph{Topological: \texttt{distal\_omission}.} A morphological opening
$\gamma_r=\delta_r\!\circ\!\varepsilon_r$ with a ball of radius $r$ removes every
structure thinner than $r$. The removable set is partitioned into connected
components, each deleted independently with probability $p$:
\begin{equation}
  \gtminus \;=\; \gtstar \;\setminus\!\!\bigcup_{c\,:\,b_c=1}\!\! C_c,
  \qquad C_c\subseteq \gtstar\setminus\gamma_r(\gtstar),
  \quad b_c\sim\text{Bernoulli}(p).
\end{equation}
This is a \emph{biased}, foreground-reducing, topology-altering noise: it deletes
distal branches. The design is deliberately \emph{radius-gated}: the opening $\gamma_r$
first identifies what counts as ``thin'' (structures the ball cannot fit through), and $p$
then decides how many of those candidate branches actually vanish. Consequently $r$ controls
\emph{which} structures are at risk and $p$ their \emph{prevalence} of loss -- a separation
that keeps the perturbation anatomically meaningful (whole terminal branches disappear, as a
hurried annotator would miss them) rather than a uniform thinning. Its matched instruments are
therefore topological (clDice, Betti$_0$); a surface metric such as NSD barely moves, which is
exactly why it serves as the flat \emph{witness} in Q1. Figures~\ref{fig:families}
and~\ref{fig:families2d} show its signature: red-only voxels ($-N/+0$), concentrated at the
periphery of the tree, and Fig.~\ref{fig:ind3d-families} renders it in isolation.

\paragraph{Surface/directional: \texttt{boundary\_drift}.} Let
$\phi(x)$ be the signed distance to $\partial\gtstar$ ($\phi<0$ inside). The boundary
is displaced by a field combining a constant drift and a smoothed zero-mean
fluctuation $\xi$ of correlation length $\ell$:
\begin{equation}
  d(x) \;=\; a\,\big(\mu + \xi(x)\big)\,g_p(x),
  \qquad a = r\,\bar{s},\quad \E[\xi]=0,
  \label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \gtminus \;=\; \{\,x:\ \phi(x)-d(x)\le 0\,\},
\end{equation}
where $\bar s$ is the mean spacing and $g_p$ a surface coverage gate selecting a
fraction $p$ of boundary voxels. Equation~\eqref{eq:drift} \textbf{decomposes the
boundary error into a systematic and a stochastic part}:
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{a\,\mu\,g_p}_{\text{systematic, \emph{learnable}}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{a\,\xi\,g_p}_{\text{zero-mean, \emph{augmentation}}}.
\end{equation}
The correlation length $\ell$ makes the fluctuation spatially smooth rather than
salt-and-pepper, matching how a real contour wanders coherently over a few voxels; the gate
$g_p$ concentrates the displacement on a top-$p$ fraction of the surface so that severity is
expressed as ``how much of the boundary moved''. Because the displacement is measured in
millimetres through the spacing ($a=r\bar s$), the same $r$ produces a physically comparable
error across anisotropic volumes. The matched instruments are the sub-voxel surface measure
NSD@0.5 for \emph{magnitude} and the signed $\Delta V$ for \emph{direction}; on thin vessels
Betti$_0$ also responds, because a boundary that recedes by the vessel radius severs the tube
(Sec.~\ref{sec:coupling}). Figures~\ref{fig:musweep} and~\ref{fig:musweep2d} show both added
(blue) and removed (red) voxels -- the fingerprint that distinguishes this family from the
purely subtractive ones (isolated 3D render in Fig.~\ref{fig:ind3d-families}).

\paragraph{Discarded controls: \texttt{distal\_truncation} and \texttt{homogeneous\_morpho}.}
Two further mechanisms were implemented and measured but excluded from the model experiments
(Fig.~\ref{fig:families}). \texttt{distal\_truncation} prunes terminal skeleton segments; it
occupies the same biased, distal, foreground-reducing axis as omission and is therefore
\emph{redundant} -- its very high clDice ($0.97$--$0.99$, Table~\ref{tab:calib-ext}) is not
evidence of harmlessness but of a \emph{mismatched instrument}, since clDice cannot see edge
filing. \texttt{homogeneous\_morpho} is a uniform erosion \emph{without} radius gating; it is
deliberately unrealistic and serves only to separate the effect of the removed volume from the
effect of the noise \emph{structure}. Keeping the family set minimal (one topological, one
surface/directional) is a methodological choice: one mechanism at a time, no composed noise,
clean causal attribution (Sec.~\ref{sec:methodology}).

\section{The bias axis $\mu$}
The single parameter $\mu$ indexes the entire experimental axis
(Fig.~\ref{fig:musweep}):
\[
  \mu<0:\ \text{under-segmentation (eroded boundary)};\quad
  \mu=0:\ \text{pure jitter};\quad
  \mu>0:\ \text{over-segmentation}.
\]
A noise is \emph{unbiased} iff $\E[\Delta V]=0$, where
$\Delta V=(|\gtminus|-|\gtstar|)/|\gtstar|$ is the signed relative volume change. Measured on
PARSE ($n{=}8$, $r{=}1,p{=}0.5$), $\E[\Delta V]$ is monotone in $\mu$:
$-0.093,-0.041,+0.015,+0.075,+0.136$ for $\mu=-1,-0.5,0,+0.5,+1$. Two honest points follow.
First, the axis behaves as designed -- $\mu$ moves the boundary systematically inward or outward.
Second, the zero of $\E[\Delta V]$ falls not exactly at $\mu=0$ but near $\mu\approx-0.1$: at
$\mu=0$ a small residual dilation bias remains ($+0.015$, i.e.\ the operator is
\emph{near}-unbiased, not exactly unbiased). We therefore speak of the $\mu=0$ point as
low-bias jitter rather than perfectly zero-mean, and read the learnable-bias regime as
$|\mu|\gtrsim0.5$, where $|\E[\Delta V]|$ is several times its residual. For $\mu\neq0$ the
systematic component is constant across realisations and can be \emph{learned}; this is the
formal bridge to the research questions of Sec.~\ref{sec:methodology}.

\section{Guiding principles}
\textbf{(i) One family at a time.} We never compose noises until each is understood in
isolation; this removes hard-to-debug confounds and gives clean causal attribution.
\textbf{(ii) Match the instrument to the quantity} -- in type, scale \emph{and}
direction (Table~\ref{tab:pairing}). \textbf{(iii) The biased/unbiased axis} organises
the questions: biased noise shifts the score (Q1) and is \emph{hypothesised} learnable (Q3, not
yet tested); near-unbiased noise is expected benign (Q2).

\section{Calibration to inter-observer agreement}\label{sec:calib}
Operating points are \emph{measured}, not assumed, on PARSE via
\texttt{calibrate\_noise.py}, each family targeting its matched metric in $[0.85,0.90]$:

\section{Research questions}
For a metric $m$ (higher better), a model $M$, and a reference $R\in\{\gtstar,\gtminus\}$,
write $m(M\mid R)$ for the per-case score; all tests are paired Wilcoxon signed-rank
($n{=}15$) with paired Cohen's $d$, and report the minimum detectable difference (MDD)
when non-significant.

\paragraph{Q1 -- Artificial penalty.} $M_0$ is evaluated against $\gtstar$ and against
$\gtminus$; \emph{only the reference changes}. A significant drop
$m(M_0\mid\gtstar)\!>\!m(M_0\mid\gtminus)$ implicates the GT, not the model.
Matched metric: clDice/Betti$_0$ for omission, $\Delta V$/NSD for drift.

\paragraph{Q2 -- Robustness / augmentation.} $M_2$ (unbiased, on-the-fly) vs.\ $M_0$,
\emph{both evaluated on clean $\gtstar$}. $\mu{=}0$ isolates robustness: with no bias
to match, any benefit is regularisation. Hypothesis $m(M_2\mid\gtstar)\ge m(M_0\mid\gtstar)$.

\paragraph{Q3 -- Learned bias (out of scope).} $M_3$ (biased, fixed) would be evaluated
against the biased $\gtminus$ and against $\gtstar$: if the bias is learned, the penalty
on the biased reference is \emph{smaller} than for $M_0$, while the deviation from
$\gtstar$ is \emph{larger} ($\Delta V(M_3)<0$). We describe the design for completeness,
but $M_3,M_4$ did not finish training in time for this submission (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion})
and no Q3 result is reported.

\section{The thin-vessel coupling}\label{sec:coupling}
On sub-millimetre tubular structures, surface and topological errors are not
separable: a boundary displacement comparable to the vessel radius pinches the vessel.
We verified (26-connectivity, $N(\gtstar){=}1$) that \texttt{boundary\_drift} genuinely
fragments the tree (Betti$_0\approx50$ at $r1\,p0.5,\mu{=}0$, rising to $143$ at $\mu{=}{-}1$;
Table~\ref{tab:calib}). This is not an artefact but a real
property of annotation error on thin structures, and it bounds what any ``surface-only''
study can claim.

\section{Data and implementation}\label{sec:data}
We use the PARSE pulmonary-artery CT dataset. Each case is a chest CT with a binary arterial
annotation at sub-millimetre in-plane resolution (spacing $\approx0.65{\times}0.65{\times}1$\,mm,
volumes $\sim512{\times}512{\times}300$); the tree is a single 26-connected component in the clean
reference. Models are trained with nnU-Net in its self-configuring \texttt{3d\_fullres}
configuration for $500$ epochs under five-fold cross-validation, which fixes the architecture and
removes it as a confound: any difference between $M_0$--$M_2$ is attributable to the training
\emph{labels}, not to model design. Degraded training regimes are applied through custom trainers
(on-the-fly resampling for $M_1,M_2$; a frozen realisation for the tier-B $M_3,M_4$). Evaluation
uses the five folds' out-of-fold validation predictions ($n{=}85$ cases in total); metrics are
computed on the native grid after cropping each prediction/reference pair to their common
bounding box, an exact optimisation (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). All degradation realisations are
seed-fixed for reproducibility.

\section{Statistical protocol and reproducibility}\label{sec:stats}
Every contrast is \emph{pre-registered}: the endpoint metric, the comparison, and the sign of
the expected effect are fixed by the design above before any score is seen
(Sec.~\ref{sec:q1}--\ref{sec:q2}). Evaluation is out-of-fold, so each case is scored by a model
that never trained on it, and the case count grows monotonically from $\sim17$ (one fold) to
$\Ncases$ (five folds) -- the \emph{anytime} property that lets the report mature without ever
re-specifying the analysis. For each contrast we form per-case paired differences and apply the
Wilcoxon signed-rank test (distribution-free), quantify magnitude with the paired Cohen's~$d$,
and control the false-discovery rate at $q{=}0.05$ (Benjamini--Hochberg) across the family of
pre-specified tests. Crucially, a non-significant result is \emph{not} reported as ``no
effect'': we compute the minimum detectable difference (MDD) at $80\%$ power for the observed
paired variance and label any gap below it \emph{undecided} -- the discipline that turns a null
into an honest statement about the instrument's resolution. Pre-specified outcome deltas also
carry a bootstrap $95\%$ confidence interval. Full definitions are in Appendix~\ref{app:stats};
the entire chain (\texttt{generate\_degraded}~$\to$~\texttt{collect\_metrics}~$\to$~%
\texttt{report}) is deterministic given the seed, so every number in this paper regenerates from
the raw predictions with a single command.

\section{Calibration and metric responses (measured; largely by construction)}
%% Numbers are final; reproduce from results/noise_calibration.csv.
The operating points are \emph{measured} on PARSE ($n{=}8$), not assumed, and each family is
calibrated on the metric matched to it. For the topological family,
\texttt{distal\_omission} at $r{=}2,\,p{=}0.3$ lands the reference in the targeted
inter-observer window on clDice, $\text{clDice}(\gtminus,\gtstar){=}0.872\in[0.85,0.90]$,
with $\text{Betti}_0{=}15$ spurious components and NSD@0.5 still high ($0.918$) --
confirming that omission is a \emph{topological}, not a surface, perturbation
(Table~\ref{tab:calib}, top).

The boundary family is where the instrument-matching argument becomes empirical. At the
operating point $r{=}1,p{=}0.5$, sweeping the bias $\mu$ from $-1$ to $+1$
(Table~\ref{tab:calib}, bottom; Fig.~\ref{fig:musweep}) dissociates the four metrics exactly as
their definitions predict:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{HD95 saturates} at the sub-voxel level ($0$--$0.66$\,mm, and exactly $0$ at
  $\mu{=}0$): below one voxel it cannot grade the drift at all.
  \item \textbf{NSD@0.5 is $\cap$-shaped and near-symmetric} about $\mu{=}0$ ($0.885$ at
  $\mu{=}{-}0.5$ vs.\ $0.869$ at $\mu{=}{+}0.5$): being \emph{unsigned}, it reads the
  \emph{magnitude} of the displacement but barely separates its \emph{direction}. This is the
  empirical justification for pairing $\mu$ with the signed $\Delta V$
  (Table~\ref{tab:pairing}).
  \item \textbf{clDice is monotone} in $\mu$ ($0.94\!\to\!0.99$): an advancing boundary
  fills the skeleton, so clDice leaks the sign but conflates it with agreement -- it is
  not a clean magnitude gauge either.
  \item \textbf{Betti}$_0$ \textbf{is monotone-decreasing} ($143\!\to\!7$): under-seg
  ($\mu{<}0$) fragments the thin tree, over-seg merges it -- the surface/topology
  coupling of Sec.~\ref{sec:coupling}. Note this is $|\Delta N|$ against the \emph{clean}
  reference ($N(\gtstar){=}1$), where it faithfully counts the degradation's fragments; the same
  statistic breaks when \emph{both} operands are fragmented, i.e.\ when it is used to
  \emph{evaluate} predictions (Sec.~\ref{sec:betti}).
\end{itemize}
No single standard metric captures both magnitude and direction; the pairing of
Table~\ref{tab:pairing} is therefore a necessity, not a preference.

\subsection{Q1 -- How large, and how coherent, is the reference effect?}
\label{sec:q1}
That a fixed model scores differently against a different reference is near-tautological -- the
distance to the reference changes when the reference changes. We therefore do not treat this as
a discovery. The two questions that carry information are (i) \emph{how large} the shift is
relative to the differences \emph{between models} (Sec.~\ref{sec:refvsmodel}), and (ii) whether
the shift is \emph{coherent} across metrics or points different directions. Table~\ref{tab:q1}
scores the clean baseline $M_0$ against $\gtstar$ and each degraded reference on the agreement
instruments matched to each family; Betti$_0$ is deliberately excluded here and analysed on its
own in Sec.~\ref{sec:betti}, because it misbehaves in this setting.

\subsection{A pathology of the Betti-$0$ error under an imperfect reference}\label{sec:betti}
The opposite-direction movement flagged above is not a curiosity; it exposes a genuine defect of
the metric in this setting. The Betti-$0$ error as commonly implemented is
$|\,N(P)-N(G)\,|$, the absolute difference in the number of $26$-connected components. This
statistic is \emph{blind to correspondence}: it compares two integers and never asks whether the
components of $P$ and $G$ are the same objects. On thin vessels the predictions are heavily
fragmented -- $N(P)$ ranges from $44$ to $81$ across cases (Table~\ref{tab:betti}) -- while the
clean tree is a single component, $N(\gtstar){=}1$. The error against $\gtstar$ is therefore
$\approx N(P)$, dominated entirely by the prediction's fragmentation. When the reference is
itself degraded, its own fragmentation $N(\gtminus)$ moves, and the error tracks the arithmetic
gap, not the anatomy.

Case \texttt{PARSE\_0021} is the reductio: a prediction with $81$ components scored against a

metric certifies as excellent a case where prediction and reference are \emph{both} badly wrong,
simply because they are wrong \emph{by the same amount}. This disqualifies $|\Delta N|$ as the
connectivity instrument for evaluation under an imperfect reference, and it explains the sign
inversion in Q1: clDice measures skeleton \emph{overlap} (a correspondence-aware quantity, which
correctly drops on a degraded reference) whereas $|\Delta N|$ measures only a count difference
(which can drop spuriously). It is worth stressing that this is our own instrument-matching
principle turning on itself: we had paired omission with ``clDice/Betti$_0$'', and the data show
that only the first half of that pairing is sound. We give the corrected recommendation and a
concrete replacement in Sec.~\ref{sec:future}.

\subsection{Q2 -- The clean/clean baseline (a sanity bound, not a finding)}\label{sec:q2}
We state this plainly: comparing $M_2$ (trained on low-bias $\mu{=}0$ jitter) against the clean
$M_0$, \emph{both scored on the clean $\gtstar$}, cannot yield a surprising result, and we do not
claim one. A model trained on degraded labels is not expected to beat a model trained on clean
labels when both are judged by clean labels; the outcome is bounded above by ``no better than
$M_0$'' almost by construction. We include the comparison only for two limited purposes: to
\emph{bound the size} of the disturbance that near-unbiased jitter causes, and to check whether
nnU-Net's already-extensive augmentation leaves any room for the ``noise-as-augmentation'' story
to hold. It does not, and we report that without inflating it into a finding.

\paragraph{Result (as expected).} $M_2$ is slightly but significantly worse than $M_0$ on the
clean reference: NSD@0.5 $0.698\!\to\!0.675$ ($\Delta{=}{-}0.023$, $d{=}{-}0.73$) and clDice
$0.877\!\to\!0.858$ ($d{=}{-}0.57$), with topology statistically indistinguishable (Betti$_0$
$81.6$ vs.\ $83.0$, $p{=}0.47$; and note Sec.~\ref{sec:betti} on reading Betti$_0$ at all). The
only non-trivial content is quantitative: the disturbance from near-unbiased jitter is bounded at
about $0.02$ on the surface metrics and is topologically negligible -- so on this anatomy, with
nnU-Net's default augmentation already in place, zero-mean boundary noise is neither harmful
enough to matter nor helpful. We draw no stronger conclusion; in particular we do \emph{not}
present this as evidence for or against ``label noise as augmentation'' in general, a claim our
single, augmentation-saturated setup cannot support.

The learned-bias question (does a model trained on a fixed biased reference internalise the
bias?) is the natural third experiment; its models were still training at submission and we
present it, honestly, as a planned direction rather than a half-reported result
(Sec.~\ref{sec:future}).

\section{The reference reverses the model ranking}\label{sec:refvsmodel}
This is the paper's central, demonstrated result, and it is a reversal, not a magnitude
comparison. \emph{Between models} on a fixed clean reference the differences are small: $M_0$
leads the two noisy-trained models by $\approx0.02$ clDice, and $M_1$ and $M_2$ are statistically
indistinguishable from each other (Table~\ref{tab:intermodel}). That smallness is exactly why what
follows matters: changing only the evaluation reference, from $\gtstar$ to the distal-omission
$\gtminus$, \emph{flips which model scores best} on two leaderboard-standard metrics -- HD95 and
NSD@2mm -- for both competitors tested against the clean-trained $M_0$.

\paragraph{The test.} For each metric, each pair of models $(A,B)$, and the transition
$\gtstar\to\gtminus_{\text{omission}}$, we build the per-case interaction
$\iota[\text{case}] = \big(m(A\mid\gtminus,\text{case})-m(B\mid\gtminus,\text{case})\big) -
\big(m(A\mid\gtstar,\text{case})-m(B\mid\gtstar,\text{case})\big)$ and test it against zero
(paired Wilcoxon signed-rank, $n{=}85$, Benjamini--Hochberg FDR over the full family of contrasts).
A reversal is declared only when the sign of $m(A\mid\cdot)-m(B\mid\cdot)$ itself differs between
$\gtstar$ and $\gtminus$ \emph{and} the interaction is significant after FDR -- a stricter criterion
than a significant interaction alone, which can just as well describe an attenuation that never
crosses zero. Applied to all six metrics and all three model pairs, eight contrasts pass the raw
criterion; four survive scrutiny (below). $d$ is reported on the raw interaction, unreoriented by
the metric's sense -- hence positive on HD95 (a $\downarrow$ metric) and negative on NSD@2 (an
$\uparrow$ metric) for the \emph{same} reversal.

The two NSD@2 rows share an identical $p_{\text{FDR}}$: not a coincidence but a floor effect --
all $85/85$ cases carry the same sign of the interaction for both pairs, so the Wilcoxon statistic
is saturated at the smallest $p$-value reachable at this $n$; we report the $85/85$ unanimity
directly rather than let two decimal-identical $p$-values imply a differentiated precision they do
not have. The reversal is not a fragile point estimate either: resampling the $85$ cases
($10\,000$ bootstrap draws) recovers a model order different from the one observed under $\gtstar$
in \emph{every single draw} for HD95 under omission (Kendall $\tau=-0.33$, bootstrap CI entirely
negative, $[-1,-0.33]$).

\paragraph{Why four, not eight.} A structurally identical contrast on $|\Delta V|$ (the unsigned
volume error) also passes the raw significance-plus-sign-flip test, on the same two model pairs
and the same omission transition, plus two more contrasts against the drift$-$ reference. Both
groups are artifacts, for two different reasons, and we report the elimination rather than the
raw count. First, re-running the same interaction test on the \emph{signed} $\Delta V$ (not its
absolute value) leaves the sign of $m(A)-m(B)$ unchanged between $\gtstar$ and $\gtminus$ for both
pairs ($+0.049\to+0.053$ and $+0.054\to+0.059$): the three models' signed volume errors shift by a
nearly identical additive offset under omission ($+0.075$, $+0.072$, $+0.072$ for $M_0,M_1,M_2$
respectively) -- a shift common to every model, which the \emph{absolute value} alone turns into a
spurious sign crossing when models sit on opposite sides of zero at different distances. This is
structurally the same defect already diagnosed for $|\Delta N|$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:betti}): under
omission, $N(\gtstar)=N(\gtminus_{\text{omission}})=1$ for every case, so the Betti-$0$ error shift
is likewise a common additive artifact that cancels exactly, case by case, in every inter-model
difference -- which is why $|\Delta N|$ never registers a reversal under omission at all, while it
does show one, visibly but not significantly ($p_{\text{FDR}}\in[0.45,0.78]$), under the more
severely fragmenting drift$-$ reference, where $N(\gtminus)$ no longer tracks $N(\gtstar)$
one-for-one. \emph{Any unsigned statistic of an intrinsically signed quantity is structurally
exposed to this failure mode}: $|\Delta N|$ and $|\Delta V|$ are two instances of one defect, not
two separate ones. Second, the two remaining $|\Delta V|$ contrasts (against drift$-$) are below
the resolution of the instrument: the minimum detectable difference at $80\%$ power
($(z_{.975}{+}z_{.80})\cdot\mathrm{sd}(\iota)/\sqrt{n}$) is $0.006$--$0.007$, an order of magnitude
above the observed $0.0002$--$0.0003$ -- undecided, by our own stated discipline of reporting MDD
rather than reading noise, not a reversal.

\paragraph{The structure is the mechanism.} All four retained reversals pit $M_0$ against one of
the noise-trained models; $M_1$ vs.\ $M_2$ never reverses, on any metric. The degraded reference
does not confuse models at random -- it specifically penalises the one trained on the clean truth.
We verified the mechanism directly rather than assert it: under omission, $M_0$ still finds the
distal branches that omission deletes from the reference; once the reference no longer contains
them, those branches score as distant false positives and inflate HD95, while $M_1$, trained on
the same omission, never predicts them either and is rewarded for reproducing the reference's own
error. Per-case, the volume omitted from the reference (relative to $\gtstar$) correlates with
$M_0$'s HD95 penalty across the $85$ cases: Pearson $r{=}{+}0.32$ (bootstrap $95\%$ CI
$[0.20,0.46]$, $p{=}0.003$), Spearman $\rho{=}{+}0.39$ ($[0.19,0.57]$, $p{=}0.0002$) -- positive
and significant, but moderate rather than overwhelming, consistent with HD95's sensitivity to
\emph{where}, not merely how much, foreground was omitted: the omitted-volume fraction is a coarse
proxy for what a distance-based metric actually integrates, the distance from the omitted
components to the rest of the reference tree, which we did not measure directly here. That
$\rho$ exceeds $r$ points to a monotone but non-linear relationship, likely driven disproportionately
by the high-omission cases rather than a uniform linear response across the range. clDice, by
contrast, is dominated by the intact proximal trunk and does not move -- worth stating plainly,
since our own instrument-pairing argument (Table~\ref{tab:pairing}) already predicted clDice would
be the metric least likely to see this.

\paragraph{Are these magnitudes real, or academic?} The reversal magnitudes are sub-voxel to
half-voxel ($0.12$--$0.37$mm HD95, spacing $\approx0.65\times0.65\times1$mm) -- our own calibration
already showed HD95 saturating below the voxel (Table~\ref{tab:calib}), so the honest question is
whether such a margin would ever separate real submissions. It would: on the PARSE 2022 challenge
leaderboard, the same anatomy and a comparable resolution ($0.5$--$0.95$mm in-plane,
$1$mm slice)~\cite{luo2023parse}, the weighted HD95 margin between the top two teams is
$0.61$mm, and between the top two on the main-PA branch alone, $0.16$mm -- the same order of
magnitude as our reversal, not beyond it and not negligibly below it. NSD is not an official PARSE
metric (only DSC and HD95 are), so our NSD@2 reversal ($0.005$--$0.007$) has no equivalent
real-leaderboard anchor and we do not claim one.

\paragraph{Robustness to unprocessed fragmentation.} None of our predictions were post-processed
by largest-connected-component (LCC) extraction ($N(P)\in[44,81]$, Sec.~\ref{sec:betti}), and HD95
is the single statistic most exposed to one isolated far-away fragment, since it reads a
percentile of the surface-distance distribution. A sceptical reading of the reversal is that it is
an artifact of this fragmentation rather than a property of the anatomy. We tested this directly:
recomputing all four contrasts of Table~\ref{tab:reversal} after applying LCC to every
prediction (never to the references, which would artificially reconnect the very branches the
omission operator removes and invalidate the scenario) leaves the reversal intact on both metrics.
HD95: $d{=}{+}0.72\!\to\!{+}0.71$ ($M_0$ vs.\ $M_1$) and $d{=}{+}0.73\!\to\!{+}0.70$ ($M_0$ vs.\
$M_2$), significant in both regimes ($p_{\text{FDR}}{<}10^{-10}$); the sign of $m(A)-m(B)$ still
flips from $\gtstar$ to $\gtminus_{\text{omission}}$ in both. NSD@2: $d{=}{-}3.14\!\to\!{-}2.67$ and
$d{=}{-}2.84\!\to\!{-}2.62$, likewise intact ($p_{\text{FDR}}{<}10^{-13}$). NSD@2's survival is
informative on its own: it is a bilateral surface-tolerance statistic, far less sensitive than a
$95^{\text{th}}$-percentile distance to a single outlier component, so a reversal that persisted on
HD95 alone but vanished on NSD@2 would have pointed to a fragment artifact -- it does not vanish,
which rules that explanation out. LCC is not free, however: it costs essentially no clDice for
$M_0$ ($0.877\!\to\!0.877$ under $\gtstar$, $0.777\!\to\!0.778$ under omission) but a real amount
for the noise-trained models ($M_1$: $0.859\!\to\!0.851$ under $\gtstar$; $M_2$:
$0.858\!\to\!0.850$), consistent with $M_1$/$M_2$ retaining more small, legitimately disconnected
distal fragments that LCC discards outright. The reversal, in short, is not a fragmentation
artifact of the unprocessed predictions -- it is a property of the anatomy and the reference,
robust to the one obvious confound a careful reviewer would raise first.

\paragraph{The ratio is context now, not the argument.} A magnitude comparison between the
reference effect and the model effect remains informative but is no longer the paper's claim: it
was an asymmetric comparison by construction, and a reversal does not need to be large to be fatal
to a benchmark ranking. Recomputed with a bootstrap CI over the $85$ cases across all
(metric, scenario, model-pair) triples, the reference-to-model effect ratio has median $2.3\times$
(IQR $[1.2\times,4.9\times]$) -- with $8$ of $54$ combinations \emph{below} $1\times$ (the model
effect exceeds the reference effect), mostly on NSD@2 and clDice under drift$+$. The old
five-to-twenty-times figure holds for some (metric, scenario, pair) triples and not others; we
report the distribution rather than a headline ratio.

whole thesis in one line: \emph{changing the reference moves the score far more than changing the
model.} A benchmark that varies the reference between submissions -- different annotators,
protocol revisions between challenge editions -- injects a nuisance larger than the signal it
means to measure.

\chapter{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================
\paragraph{Threats to validity.} We consider the main ways our reading could be wrong.
\emph{Construct validity}: our noise is simulated, so a sceptic may argue it does not match real
annotator behaviour. We mitigate this by calibrating each operating point to a measured
inter-observer agreement window rather than choosing severities by eye, but the shape of the
noise (single family, smooth field) remains a model. \emph{Internal validity}: because $M_1$ and
$M_2$ differ from $M_0$ only in their training labels and share nnU-Net's fixed configuration, a
difference among them is attributable to the labels, not the architecture -- but the on-the-fly
vs.\ fixed regime distinction (Q2 vs.\ Q3) is a deliberate confound with the noise type, and we
therefore never compare across it. \emph{Statistical validity}: with $n{=}85$ paired cases and
FDR control the Q1 effects are far from the significance boundary ($d$ up to $3$), so they are
robust to reasonable analytic choices; the Q2 effect is smaller and, while significant, should be
read as ``a small real cost'', not a dramatic one. \emph{External validity}: a single anatomy and
architecture bound generalisation, as Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations} states plainly. None of these
threatens the paper's core claim, which is conditional and methodological: \emph{given} that a
reference can be biased, here is how much that can move a verdict, and here is how to measure it.

\paragraph{A metrological motivation.} For $\mu{=}0$ we expect the model-to-model
difference to be small, possibly below metric sensitivity. We therefore cannot
distinguish ``no effect'' from ``effect below resolution''. This is precisely why the
\emph{sensitivity} of the metrics themselves (their minimum detectable difference) must
be studied alongside the models -- a measurement cannot be trusted beyond the
resolution of its instrument. The calibration sweep already exhibits this in miniature:
HD95 was flat across the entire $\mu$ range (Table~\ref{tab:calib}), so any model
comparison expressed in HD95 on this data would be reading noise. The instrument, not the
model, would be doing the ranking.

\paragraph{Which noise mis-ranks models.} Our design separates two effects that the
literature often conflates: how a noise \emph{deforms a reference} (metric level) and how
it \emph{separates trained models} (model level). Biased noise (omission, drift
$\mu\!\neq\!0$) acts on the reference (Q1, measured) and is \emph{conjectured} to act on the
model too (Q3, left to future work) -- while near-unbiased jitter deforms a single realisation of
the reference yet, averaged over resamplings, left little trainable trace on this setup (Q2). The practical corollary is
that the dangerous annotation errors for benchmarking are the \emph{systematic} ones --
exactly the ones a single annotator or a single labelling protocol is most likely to
introduce, and exactly the ones an unbiased-noise robustness argument does \emph{not}
cover.

\paragraph{The augmentation that was not.} The Q2 finding refutes a comfortable default -- that
label noise ``regularises'' and so justifies sloppy annotation. Crucially, the small cost we
measured ($\Delta$NSD@0.5${=}{-}0.023$) was comfortably resolved by NSD@0.5 but sat well inside
the saturation range of the coarser NSD@2\,mm and HD95 (Table~\ref{tab:calib}). The same
experiment read on those metrics would have concluded ``augmentation confirmed, no cost'' -- a
direct illustration of why the instrument must be chosen before the claim, and of how ``no
harm'' and ``benefit'' are different statements only one of which survived.

\paragraph{Why thin vessels are the stress test.} The surface/topology coupling
(Sec.~\ref{sec:coupling}) is not a flaw of our operator but a property of the anatomy:
when the vessel radius approaches the annotation uncertainty, a boundary error and a
topological error are the same event. Any evaluation that reports only a surface metric
on such structures is therefore blind to half of what it measures. Pulmonary arteries are
an extreme, but the same regime recurs wherever structures are thin relative to voxel
size -- airways, small vessels, nerves, trabeculae.

\paragraph{Generalisation and scope.} We deliberately trade breadth for control: one
dataset, one architecture, one family at a time. The formal operator, the bias axis
$\mu$, and the instrument-matching protocol are dataset-agnostic and transfer directly;
the specific operating points and effect sizes do not, and should be re-calibrated per
anatomy and per acquisition. What we claim to establish is a \emph{method} for asking
whether a ranking survives its reference, not a universal constant.

\chapter{Ethical Inquiry}\label{sec:ethics}
% =====================================================================
A benchmark is an epistemic instrument: it produces the evidence on which the community
allocates trust, citations, funding and -- when a model reaches the clinic -- patient risk. We
keep this section short and strictly tied to what we measured; the speculative reach of an
earlier draft has been cut.

\section{What the measurements license}
One claim follows directly from the data. Because the reference effect dominates the model effect
(Sec.~\ref{sec:refvsmodel}) and is not even coherent across metrics, a ranking computed against an
imperfect reference can reflect the annotation convention as much as the algorithm. When two
models are separated by less than the reference-induced shift -- routine, given that shift reached
$0.10$ clDice here against sub-point published margins -- selecting or deploying the ``winner'' is
a decision partly made by the annotator, and invisibly so, because the reference is treated as
truth rather than estimate. Reporting a point score without the reference's uncertainty therefore
overstates the evidence; in a field feeding clinical tools that is an ethical issue, not only a
statistical one. The Betti-$0$ pathology (Sec.~\ref{sec:betti}) sharpens the same point at the
level of a single metric: a widely used connectivity score can certify two mutually wrong objects
as topologically excellent, so an unexamined metric can manufacture false confidence on its own.

\section{What they do \emph{not} license}
We are explicit about the boundary. The strongest ethical charge one could level here -- that a
model \emph{learns} a systematic annotation bias and thereby automates human error at
population scale -- is \emph{not} something we have measured; the learned-bias experiment is
still pending (Sec.~\ref{sec:future}). We therefore make no such claim. Nor do we claim anything
about the bias of any specific real benchmark: our noise is simulated and calibrated to a
literature window, not to a measured second annotator on PARSE. The ethics we assert is
\emph{conditional}: given that references are imperfect -- which we quantify -- reporting must
change; whether bias is also learnable is a question we have set up but left open.

\section{Recommendations, grounded in the results}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item Report the reference's uncertainty (inter-observer agreement) and the metric's minimum
  detectable difference; treat any model gap below either as \emph{undecided}. A ``too close to
  call at this reference quality'' is a legitimate result.
  \item Match the metric to the claim, and \emph{audit the metric itself}: our data disqualify
  $|\Delta N|$ as a connectivity score under imperfect references and favour a
  correspondence-aware alternative (Sec.~\ref{sec:future}).
  \item Prefer multi-annotator or bias-audited references for any benchmark used to select a model
  for clinical use, and document annotation provenance as first-class metadata, since it bounds
  the bias term $\mu$ no model engineering can undo.
\end{enumerate}

% =====================================================================
\chapter{Directions}\label{sec:future}
% =====================================================================
We separate what we can already recommend from what we can only propose.

\paragraph{A correspondence-aware topological metric (concrete).} The Betti-$0$ pathology of
Sec.~\ref{sec:betti} has a clear cause -- $|\Delta N|$ compares counts, not components -- and a
clear remedy: measure topology in a way that pairs structures before scoring them. Two options
are practical. (i) \emph{clDice} is already correspondence-aware (it tests whether one skeleton
lies inside the other volume) and, in our data, behaved correctly where $|\Delta N|$ did not; we
recommend it as the default connectivity instrument under imperfect references. (ii) For a
signed topological error, a \emph{persistent-homology} Betti-number computed after matching
components by spatial overlap (e.g.\ a Hungarian assignment on the connected-component graphs)
would report unmatched births/deaths rather than a bare count difference, and would not collapse
for two mutually fragmented masks. Re-running our Q1 analysis with such a metric is the immediate
next step and requires no new data.

\paragraph{The learned-bias experiment (out of scope, left to future work).} Whether a model
trained on a fixed, systematically biased reference \emph{internalises} that bias is a sharp
question our framework poses but does not answer here: the two fixed-bias models did not complete
training in time for this submission (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}). A cheap probe -- fine-tuning the
clean-trained model on a frozen biased reference for a short budget and checking whether $\Delta V$
drifts in the direction of the bias -- would cost a fraction of the pre-registered design and could
motivate a properly powered follow-up, but does not itself belong in this paper.

\paragraph{A distance-based proxy for the reversal mechanism.} Sec.~\ref{sec:refvsmodel} correlates
the \emph{volume} omitted from the reference with $M_0$'s HD95 penalty ($\rho{=}0.39$) as a
first, coarse test of the mechanism; a distance metric should correlate with a tighter, more
linear proxy -- the maximum distance from an omitted component to the rest of the reference tree,
which is what HD95 actually integrates and volume only approximates. We flag this as the natural
next refinement, not yet computed.

\paragraph{Grounding the operator in real annotations.} Our strongest structural assumption is
that a calibrated synthetic operator stands in for real annotator disagreement
(Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}). The decisive validation is to acquire a second, independent tracing
of a PARSE subset, measure the true inter-observer $\Delta V$, clDice and NSD, and check that a
setting of $\theta$ reproduces them -- not just their central value but their spatial structure.
This would convert every ``within-IAA'' statement in this paper from calibrated-to-literature to
calibrated-to-data.

\chapter{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% =====================================================================
We list the boundaries of the study so that its claims are read at the right scope.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Simulated, not observed, noise.} $\Nop$ is calibrated to an inter-observer
  agreement window but is a model of annotation error, not a sample of it. Real annotator
  populations may combine families and exhibit spatially structured bias our single-family
  design does not reproduce. Composed noises are deferred by construction
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:methodology}).
  \item \textbf{One anatomy, one dataset, one architecture.} Results are on PARSE pulmonary
  arteries with nnU-Net at a fixed configuration. The operator and protocol transfer;
  operating points and effect sizes must be re-calibrated elsewhere.
  \item \textbf{Sample size and power.} With $n{=}\Ncases$ OOF cases the paired tests have
  limited power for small effects; this is why non-significant contrasts are reported with an
  MDD rather than as null. The $\mu{=}0$ regime (Q2) is expected to sit near that resolution
  limit.
  \item \textbf{The surface/topology coupling is a confound by nature.} On thin vessels we
  cannot fully isolate a pure surface effect from a topological one; we treat this as a real
  property to be reported jointly, not a nuisance to be removed, but it does bound
  single-metric interpretation.
  \item \textbf{Learned bias (Q3) out of scope.} The fixed-bias models ($M_3,M_4$) that would
  test whether a network learns a systematic annotation bias did not complete training in time
  for this submission ($M_3$: one fold, stopped at epoch 95/500, no recoverable checkpoint;
  $M_4$: training never started). We leave this question, and its fixed-realisation design
  (Table~\ref{tab:models}), to future work.
  \item \textbf{Metric set.} We study four metrics chosen for the pairing argument; other
  instruments (e.g.\ boundary IoU, centreline-length ratios) may resolve regimes ours cannot.
\end{itemize}

% =====================================================================
\chapter{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================
We framed annotation noise as a formal, calibrated operator with a single bias axis,
turned three intuitive questions into pre-registered, instrument-matched tests, and
surfaced metrological limits -- realistic boundary error is sub-clinical for standard
metrics, and surface and topology are inseparable on thin vessels. The numbers bear the
argument out: on $85$ out-of-fold cases the artificial evaluation penalty (Q1) is large and
unambiguous ($\Delta\text{clDice}{=}{-}0.10$, $d{=}{-}3.06$), while the comfortable assumption
that unbiased noise ``augments'' (Q2) did not hold -- it cost a little and gained nothing on
this anatomy. Whether a systematic bias is itself learnable by the network (Q3) is left to
future work. Beyond any single number, the work is a plea for measurement
humility in segmentation: a model is only as trustworthy as the reference and the metric we
judge it by, and both must be reported, matched, and doubted with the same rigour we spend on
the model.

\chapter{Formal metric definitions}\label{app:defs}
Let $P,G\subseteq\Omega$ be predicted and reference foreground sets on a grid with spacing
$s$. Write $\partial X$ for the surface (voxels of $X$ with a background 6-neighbour),
$\mathrm{Sk}(X)$ for the morphological skeleton, and $N(X)$ for the number of
26-connected components.

\paragraph{clDice (centreline Dice, $\uparrow$).} With topological precision and
sensitivity
\[
  T_{\text{prec}}=\frac{|\mathrm{Sk}(P)\cap G|}{|\mathrm{Sk}(P)|},\qquad
  T_{\text{sens}}=\frac{|\mathrm{Sk}(G)\cap P|}{|\mathrm{Sk}(G)|},
\]
$\text{clDice}=2\,T_{\text{prec}}T_{\text{sens}}/(T_{\text{prec}}+T_{\text{sens}})$.
A break in a tube is penalised even if it removes few voxels; a boundary shift that leaves
the centreline intact is not -- the source of clDice's blindness to drift.

\paragraph{NSD@$\tau$ (Normalised Surface Dice, $\uparrow$).} With $d(x,Y)=\min_{y\in Y}\|x-y\|_s$,
\[
  \text{NSD}_\tau=\frac{|\{x\in\partial P: d(x,\partial G)\le\tau\}|+|\{y\in\partial G: d(y,\partial P)\le\tau\}|}{|\partial P|+|\partial G|}.
\]
We use $\tau=0.5$\,mm (sub-voxel, sensitive to drift) and $\tau=2$\,mm (clinical tolerance).
NSD is \emph{unsigned}: it cannot distinguish an inward from an outward displacement.

\paragraph{HD95 ($\downarrow$, mm).} The $95$th percentile of
$\{d(x,\partial G):x\in\partial P\}\cup\{d(y,\partial P):y\in\partial G\}$. Robust to $5\%$
outliers; saturates below one voxel.

\paragraph{Betti$_0$ error ($\downarrow$).} $|N(P)-N(G)|$, counting fragmentations and
fusions. Computed in 26-connectivity, for which $N(\gtstar){=}1$ on the connected arterial
tree (6-connectivity spuriously reports $\sim99$).

\paragraph{Signed volume change ($\Delta V$).} $\Delta V=(|P|-|G|)/|G|$. The only signed
instrument in our set: $\Delta V<0$ under-segmentation, $\Delta V>0$ over-segmentation;
monotone in the bias $\mu$, hence the matched direction gauge (Table~\ref{tab:pairing}).

\chapter{Degradation algorithms}\label{app:algos}
Both operators are deterministic given $(\varphi,r,p,\mu,\text{seed})$.

\paragraph{\texttt{distal\_omission}$(G;r,p)$.} (i) Opening $\gamma_r=\delta_r\circ\varepsilon_r$
with a ball of radius $r$; (ii) removable set $R=G\setminus\gamma_r(G)$ (the structures thinner
than $r$); (iii) partition $R$ into connected components $\{C_c\}$; (iv) draw
$b_c\sim\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ and set $\gtminus=G\setminus\bigcup_{c:b_c=1}C_c$; (v) if $p>0$ and
no component was drawn, force the removal of one (the ``at least one'' guarantee). Biased,
foreground-reducing, topology-altering.

\paragraph{\texttt{boundary\_drift}$(G;r,p,\mu)$.} (i) Signed distance $\phi=\text{SDF}(\partial G)$,
$\phi<0$ inside; (ii) a zero-mean Gaussian field smoothed to correlation length $\ell$ gives the
fluctuation $\xi$, $\E[\xi]=0$; (iii) a coverage gate $g_p$ selects the top-$p$ fraction of
surface voxels; (iv) displacement $d(x)=a(\mu+\xi(x))g_p(x)$ with amplitude $a=r\bar s$;
(v) $\gtminus=\{x:\phi(x)-d(x)\le 0\}$. By design the systematic part $a\mu g_p$ is a
stationary target (in principle learnable) and the zero-mean part $a\xi g_p$ varies per
realisation (augmentation-like); whether these design intents hold empirically is the object of
Q2/Q3.

\chapter{Statistical protocol}\label{app:stats}
Evaluation is \emph{out-of-fold}: each of five nnU-Net folds predicts the $\sim17$ validation
cases it never trained on; their union covers all $\Ncases$ cases without leakage, and $n$ grows
monotonically as folds finish (the \emph{anytime} property). For each pre-registered contrast we
compute per-case paired differences and test $H_0:\text{median}=0$ with the Wilcoxon signed-rank
test (no normality assumption); effect size is the paired Cohen's $d=\overline{\Delta}/s_\Delta$.
Across the family of pre-specified tests we control the false discovery rate at $q{=}0.05$
(Benjamini--Hochberg). When a contrast is non-significant we report the minimum detectable
difference (MDD) at $80\%$ power given the observed paired variance, and interpret any
model gap below the MDD as \emph{undecided} rather than null. Pre-specified outcome deltas
additionally carry a bootstrap $95\%$ confidence interval. Everything is regenerated from the
master table by \texttt{report.py}; results are watermarked \textsc{preliminary} until all folds
are in.

\chapter{Reproducibility}\label{app:repro}
Every figure and number regenerates from the raw nnU-Net predictions with a fixed seed.
The pipeline is: (i) \texttt{generate\_degraded\_dataset.py --seed 42} materialises the
deterministic evaluation references $\gtminus$ for each scenario; (ii)
\texttt{collect\_metrics.py} (or its parallel variant, which crops each prediction/reference
pair to their common bounding box -- an exact optimisation verified to reproduce every metric to
machine precision) writes the long-format master table \texttt{results/metrics.csv}, one row per
$(\text{model},\text{fold},\text{scenario},\text{case})$ carrying its provenance (epochs,
checkpoint time, git commit, nnU-Net version); (iii) \texttt{report.py} emits the paired-Wilcoxon
tables (\texttt{metrics\_stats\_oof.csv}), the pre-specified outcomes with bootstrap CIs
(\texttt{outcomes\_oof.csv}), the summary figures, and a \texttt{STATUS.md} dashboard watermarked
by fold coverage. Evaluation is out-of-fold, so no case is scored by a model that trained on it;
the master table grows monotonically as folds complete, and re-running any stage only computes
the missing keys (idempotent upsert). All results in this paper are tier-A ($M_0,M_1,M_2$,
$n{=}85$, all five folds); the tier-B fixed-bias models ($M_3,M_4$) did not complete training
in time for this submission and no tier-B numbers are reported (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).
